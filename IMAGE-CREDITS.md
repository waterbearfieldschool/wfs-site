and licence.

## ws-coldframe.jpg
Cold frames with straw insulation mats, figure 45 from Charles Baltet,
*La pépinière* (1897). Engraving signed G. Johannet.

- Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Baltet_-_La_p%C3%A9pini%C3%A8re_-_fig45.jpg
- Licence: **public domain** (published 1897; author died 1908)
- Changes: cropped to 700×394 from the original 960×825, biased toward the top to
  keep both frames and the straw mats in view.

Attribution is not legally required for a public-domain work, but it is recorded
here because it should be, and because the date in the engraving is part of why
we chose it.

## ws-knots.jpg
Plate of knots, *Nordisk familjebok* (1911), vol. 14, p. 402. Engraving signed "JH";
artist otherwise unknown.

- Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Nf_knots.png
- Licence: **public domain** (confirmed via the Commons API — `Copyrighted: False`,
  `UsageTerms: Public domain`, dated 1911)
- Changes: fitted onto a 700×394 white field from the original 339×333 (1.18× upscale,
  whole plate preserved; the white padding is invisible against the engraving's own
  white ground).

The knots shown, per the Commons description — useful if we ever caption it or build
the session around the plate:

1. Yarn knot (ABoK #2688) · 2. Manrope knot · 3. Granny knot · 4. Rosebud stopper knot (?)
· 5. Matthew Walker knot · 6. Shroud knot · 7. Turk's head knot · 8. Overhand knot and
figure-eight knot · 9. Square knot · 10. Two half-hitches

## ws-plantid.jpg
Botanical reference plate ("Plante / planche"), *Larousse du XXe siècle* (1932) —
roots, stems, leaves, inflorescences, flowers and fruits, each specimen labelled.

- Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Plante_planche_-_Plants,_botanical_illustrations_-_Public_domain_illustration_from_Larousse_du_XX%C3%A8me_si%C3%A8cle_1932.jpg
- Licence: **CC0** (Creative Commons Zero, public-domain dedication; the 1932 plate is
  also public domain by age). Confirmed via the Commons API.
- Changes: cropped the top band (roots / whole plant / stem, including the trunk
  cross-section) and scaled to 700×394. The full plate is portrait and far too dense to
  read at card size — the band keeps the specimens legible.

## ws-foldingtable.jpg
"Design for Mahogany Folding Card Table, 1900–05" — a pencil working drawing with
handwritten dimensions, Cooper Hewitt, Smithsonian Design Museum (CH 18681677).
Artist unknown.

- Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Drawing,_Design_for_Mahogany_Folding_Card_Table,_1900%E2%80%9305_(CH_18681677).jpg
- Licence: **public domain**. Confirmed via the Commons API.
- Changes: cropped to the table itself from the original 3130×2847 and scaled to 700×394,
  keeping the "Cloth Top" note, the 2'6¼" × 33" dimensions and the 26" height line.

*Not a workbench — it is a folding card table. Chosen because it is a real shop
drawing with real dimensions, which is what the session is about.*
